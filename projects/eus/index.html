EuS, from First Principles

Europium sulfide is a ferromagnetic semiconductor built on the half-filled 4f shell of Eu2+, exactly the kind of strongly localized electrons standard density functional theory struggles with. These are my Quantum ESPRESSO results: what plain PBE predicts, how much a Hubbard U on the 4f shell fixes, and what it cannot.

The EuGdCuS3 nanorods from my thesis owe their magnetism to the same Eu2+ 4f7 shell. Each calculation here takes minutes to hours on a laptop, so this page explores the computed results rather than rerunning them in the browser.

Why 1.65 eV is the wrong target for these gaps

A Hubbard U penalizes fractional occupation of the 4f shell, pushing the occupied 4f states down and the empty ones up. The gap barely moves until U reaches about 4 eV, then grows steadily.

The measured gap is the 4f → 5d edge: in real EuS the occupied 4f level sits above the S 3p band and forms the top of the valence band. In PBE+U that ordering only holds at small U. As U grows the 4f sinks below the valence band top, which becomes S 3p, so the computed gap turns into an S 3p → Eu 5d gap, a different transition.

So no single U gets both the gap and the 4f position right. A small U keeps the 4f on top but leaves almost no gap; a large U opens a gap only by burying the 4f. Getting both needs a method that also corrects the Eu 5d conduction band, such as a hybrid functional or GW.

More on the convergence tests

The absolute energy converges too slowly to be a useful criterion here. At 60 Ry the pressure is −1216 kbar, off the chart: Pulay stress from an incomplete basis. All tests use PBE+U with U = 7 eV.

At the experimental lattice constant the converged pressure is +6.6 kbar. That sign is a check in itself: the PBE+U equilibrium lattice constant (6.000 Å) is larger, so the cell is slightly compressed there, and with a 50 GPa bulk modulus a 0.5% compression corresponds to about +8 kbar.

Next: from exchange constants to Tc

The Eu2+ spins sit on an fcc lattice, coupled to their 12 nearest neighbors by J1 and their 6 next-nearest neighbors by J2. Total energies of the ferromagnetic state and two antiferromagnetic orderings, alternating (001) planes and alternating (111) planes, separate the two. The result will feed the Monte Carlo simulation, once it is extended to the fcc lattice, for a predicted Curie temperature to set against the measured 16.5 K.
