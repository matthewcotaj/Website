A hard acid, a soft base, and three years of getting them to agree

Trivalent lanthanides are hard Lewis acids. Chalcogenides are soft bases. That mismatch is why colloidal Ln–S chemistry stays stubborn — and why the reaction would rather hand you an oxysulfide than the phase you asked for.

My thesis works around it with air-stable single-source dithiocarbamate precursors, an HSAB-informed choice of copper source, and a degassing protocol strict enough to keep EuOS out of the pattern. The payoff is a lanthanide sublattice you can rewrite: swap Gd³⁺ for half the Eu²⁺ and the magnetism follows.

Cu⁺ from CuI is a soft acid, well matched to the sulfur donors of the DTC ligand and the growing sulfide lattice, where Cu²⁺ from Cu(acac)₂ is not; the iodide released during reaction adsorbs selectively on specific crystal faces and directs the anisotropic growth. Half-substituting Eu(DTC)₃(phen) with the Gd analogue gives EuGdCuS₃ on the same route.

Why the substitution works

Two sites, seven electrons

Eu₂CuS₃ has two crystallographically distinct lanthanide sites. Only one of them is doing any magnetic work.

Eu²⁺ sits at the seven-coordinate Eu(2) site: 4f⁷, half-filled, S = 7/2, and it carries the moment. Eu³⁺ sits at the six-coordinate Eu(1) site with a 4f⁶ configuration whose J = 0 ground state gives no first-order moment — a structural spectator. Gd³⁺ is also 4f⁷. Putting it on the Eu(1) site swaps the spectator for a second spin-active sublattice, which is why the magnetization goes up without the framework changing.

Three of the seven 4f orbitals

Eu³⁺ · 4f⁶Eu(1) site · the spectator
⁷F₀L = 3, S = 3, J = 0 — no first-order moment. Contributes nothing to the ordered magnetization.
Eu²⁺ and Gd³⁺ · 4f⁷Eu(2) and Eu(1) sites · the carriers
⁸S7/2Half-filled shell: L = 0, S = 7/2, a 7.94 μB spin-only effective moment and 7 μB at saturation. Both ions carry it identically.

Direct 4f–4f overlap between neighboring Eu²⁺ ions is negligible, so coupling has to run the long way round: the 4f spin polarizes a bridging S 3p orbital, passes through the diamagnetic Cu 3d¹⁰ sublattice acting as a ligand bridge rather than a spin carrier, and out through a second S 3p bridge. A long chain of filled intermediate orbitals makes for weak exchange — and a correspondingly low ordering temperature.

Intellectual interests

The reading that pulls me forward

Where the curiosity actually sits — concentrated in the physics and chemistry of correlated and magnetic matter, with a persistent side interest in how markets price risk.

Quantum information systems

Superconducting (transmon) and spin qubits, defect color centers, rare-earth-ion and molecular spin qubits; coherence and decoherence, spin-lattice (T₁) and spin-spin (T₂) relaxation, hyperfine and spin–orbit coupling, clock transitions and decoherence-free subspaces; entanglement and the DiVincenzo criteria, cavity and circuit QED coupling, error correction and surface codes — and the materials chemistry underneath platforms such as IBM Quantum.

Quantum materials

BCS and unconventional (cuprate, iron-based) superconductivity and Cooper pairing; band topology, Berry curvature, Chern numbers and Z₂ invariants in topological insulators and Weyl and Dirac semimetals; charge- and spin-density-wave order and the Peierls instability; kagome lattices, geometric frustration, flat bands and van Hove singularities; the Hubbard model, Mott insulators, Kondo physics and heavy fermions; quantum criticality and emergent phases from competing orders.

Magnetism & magnetic materials

Direct exchange, superexchange and the Goodenough–Kanamori rules, double exchange, RKKY and Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interactions; the Heisenberg and Ising models and mean-field theory; ferro-, antiferro- and ferrimagnetic ordering with their Curie and Néel transitions; rare-earth 4f magnetism, crystal-field splitting, Hund’s rules and single-ion anisotropy; magnetocrystalline anisotropy and spin–orbit coupling; magneto-optical Faraday and Kerr effects and magnetic circular dichroism; frustration, and the resolution of magnetic structure by neutron diffraction.

Synthesis, structure & property relationships

Solid-state and colloidal synthesis, LaMer nucleation and growth, and single-source precursor design; thermodynamic versus kinetic control and phase-diagram reasoning; crystal-field and ligand-field theory; cation site substitution, aliovalent doping and Vegard’s law; point-defect chemistry in Kröger–Vink terms; and the Goldschmidt tolerance factor and related structure–property descriptors linking composition to electronic, magnetic and optical behavior.

Nanoelectronics & spintronics

Giant and tunnel magnetoresistance, magnetic tunnel junctions and spin transport; spin-transfer and spin–orbit torque, the Rashba effect and the spin Hall effect; spin injection and spin-diffusion length; and the interfacial phenomena in thin-film heterostructures that couple magnetic order to charge transport.

Energy storage & conversion

Electrochemical intercalation and ion transport, the redox chemistry of electrode materials, ionic conductivity in solid-state electrolytes, and overpotential and electrocatalysis in energy conversion.

Financial markets & analysis

Discounted-cash-flow and dividend-discount valuation, relative and multiples-based valuation, and fundamental and financial-statement analysis; the efficient-market hypothesis and its limits; modern portfolio theory and mean-variance optimization; and CAPM, arbitrage pricing theory and multi-factor (Fama–French) models linking risk to expected return.

Trading & market behavior

Market microstructure, price formation, liquidity and the bid–ask spread; the random-walk hypothesis, momentum and mean reversion; implied and realized volatility and Black–Scholes option pricing; and behavioral finance, prospect theory and cognitive biases — approached semi-quantitatively and analytically rather than algorithmically.
